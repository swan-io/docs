# Card payments

<p className="ia-lede">How card <Term id="transactions">transactions</Term> work at Swan: categories, authorization and clearing, 3-D Secure, international <Term id="payments">payments</Term>, fraud, and enriched merchant data. For the cards themselves, from formats and issuing to settings, refer to the [Cards section](/cards).</p>

## Card transactions {#transactions}

Each card transaction carries the payment data of the [`CardTransaction` interface](https://api-reference.swan.io/objects/card-transaction/): the merchant's terminal ID and merchant ID (unique identifiers in the Mastercard network), name, city, postal code, and Merchant Category Code (MCC), plus the original amount and currency.

Card transactions are categorized based on where and how the payment was made.
The `CardTransactionCategory` enum provides the following values:

- **InStore**: Point-of-sale terminal transactions made at a physical merchant location.
- **eCommerce**: Online transactions without 3-D Secure <Term id="consent">consent</Term>.
- **eCommerceWith3DS**: Online transactions with 3-D Secure consent.
- **Withdrawal**: ATM withdrawals.
- **Other**: Transactions that don't fit the above categories, including non-standard merchant flows and technical retries.

Card transactions move through their own [card transaction statuses](/payments/concepts/cards/statuses).
When a card payment is rejected, the account isn't charged, and the `reason` in the rejected transaction's `statusInfo` explains why: refer to the [card rejection reasons](/payments/reference/card-rejection-reasons) for the common codes and resolutions to share with your users.

## Processing card payments {#processing}

Card payments are first [authorized, then cleared](/payments/concepts/cards/authorization-and-clearing): the authorization reserves funds as a `Pending` transaction, and the clearing debits them a few days later, with several capture types and merchant flows.
Online payments may also require [3-D Secure](/payments/concepts/cards/3ds) authentication before the authorization is accepted.
After the transaction is created, Swan adds [enriched transaction information](/payments/concepts/cards/enriched-info) about the merchant.

## Contactless payments {#contactless}

Physical cards have contactless limits before a PIN is required, and digital wallet payments bypass them.
Refer to [contactless payments](/cards/concepts/physical#contactless) in the physical cards concept.

## Fraud and card transactions {#fraud}

When a cardholder is a victim of fraud, they must declare it to Swan.
If, after an internal investigation, the chargeback is admissible, Swan creates a new `InternalCreditTransferIn` credit transaction with the [status `Booked`](/payments/concepts/cards/statuses#statuses), which impacts the [account's booked balance](/accounts/concepts/account/balances).
The chargeback transaction is linked to the same payment as the initial card transaction.

At the slightest suspicion of fraud, [block eCommerce payments on the card product](/cards/guides/lifecycle/update/using-the-api#guide), then block or cancel the affected cards.

## International card transactions {#international}

To let cardholders make international card transactions, allow them in the [card settings](/cards/reference/card-settings#settings).
For a smoother user experience, both `international` and `nonMainCurrencyTransactions` should be activated.

Unless your Terms and Conditions state differently, a **2% fee** applies to international card transactions.
[Mastercard's exchange rate](https://www.mastercard.com/content/mastercardcom/global/en/personal/get-support/convert-currency.html) applies to card transactions outside of [SEPA](/payments/reference/sepa#sepa).
The [card payment countries](/payments/reference/card-payment-countries) reference lists where card payments are allowed or blocked.
