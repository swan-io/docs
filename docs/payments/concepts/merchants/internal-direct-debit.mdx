# Internal Direct Debit for merchants

<p className="ia-lede">Accept <Term id="payments">payments</Term> between Swan accounts in the same project with <Term id="direct-debit">Internal Direct Debit</Term>: the Standard and B2B schemes, mandates and their signature, settlement, and <Term id="r-transaction">R-transactions</Term>.</p>

## Overview {#overview}

<Term id="merchants">Merchants</Term> can use Internal Direct Debit to accept payments when the merchant and the debtor are using Swan accounts within the same Swan project.
Swan plays the role of the merchant bank, the clearing and settlement mechanism, and the debtor bank.

If the merchant and debtor aren't using Swan accounts within the same Swan project, use [SEPA Direct Debit for merchants](/payments/concepts/merchants/sepa-direct-debit) instead.

## Schemes: Standard and B2B {#schemes}

Swan offers two Internal Direct Debit schemes: `Standard` and `B2B`.
You can choose to use one or both schemes according to your use case.

The [Internal Direct Debit scheme table](/payments/reference/schemes#idd) compares eligible account types, refunds, returns, mandate requirements, and timelines for Standard and B2B.

## Rolling reserve {#rolling-reserve}

[Rolling reserve](/payments/concepts/merchants/payments#rolling-reserve) applies to the Standard scheme, with a reserve determined by a <Term id="merchant-profile">merchant profile</Term> risk assessment. The B2B scheme doesn't require a <Term id="rolling-reserve">rolling reserve</Term>.

## Canceling payments {#canceling}

Partners can cancel Internal Direct Debit payments using the [`cancelTransaction`](https://api-reference.swan.io/mutations/cancel-transaction/) mutation. Only the full transaction amount can be canceled.

Payments can only be canceled before <Term id="transactions">transactions</Term> are processed, which is typically before 12:00 Central European [Summer] Time (CET/CEST) one business day before the execution date.
After this time, the payment can't be canceled and will proceed to settlement.

## Payment mandates {#mandates}

<Term id="payment-mandates">Payment mandates</Term> are mandatory for B2B Internal Direct Debit transactions and optional but recommended for Standard.
Partners obtain them, have them signed, and declare them to Swan.
Refer to [merchant payment mandates](/payments/concepts/merchants/mandates#idd) for the required information, sample content, signature rules, received mandates, and one-off mandates.

## Settlement date and booked time {#settlement}

Internal Direct Debit transactions are **booked at 18:00 Central European [Summer] Time (CET/CEST)** on the settlement date.

For both B2B and Standard, the settlement date is one day after (D+1) initiating the debit if transactions are sent before **12 noon**.

## R-transactions {#r-transactions}

:::caution R-transaction availability
Returns and refunds are only possible with **Standard** and can be requested up to 8 calendar weeks after due date and time.
:::

### Rejected {#rejected}

When an Internal Direct Debit Standard transaction is **rejected**, the debtor's account is not debited.

The [Internal Direct Debit reasons](/payments/reference/merchant-rejection-reasons#idd) list each code and the resolution to share with your merchant.

Regardless of the reason, the merchant receives a notification and can attempt a new Internal Direct Debit at a later date.

### Returned {#returned}

When an Internal Direct Debit Standard transaction is **returned**, the debtor's account is debited the amount, but the payment is reversed and the funds return to the debtor's account. The original transaction stays `Booked`; Swan creates a new return transaction that debits the merchant's account.

The [Internal Direct Debit reasons](/payments/reference/merchant-rejection-reasons#idd) mark which codes apply to returns.

Regardless of the reason, the merchant is notified about the return.
They can investigate the reason for return and take whatever action is necessary, which might be refunding the payment or disputing the return with the debtor's bank.
